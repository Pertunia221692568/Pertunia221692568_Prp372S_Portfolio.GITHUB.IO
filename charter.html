Agile Team Charter

A living social contract that defined our team’s shared purpose, values, working agreements and boundaries, so that daily work stayed aligned.

Evidence 10 marks

Our charter was written by Group 34 at the start of the project and last updated on 21 April 2026.

Team mission

To develop LaunchPad.dev, a student-focused career acceleration platform that helps final-year ICT students move from academic learning to employment.

Definition of success

  • All deliverables completed before the deadlines
  • A realistic, structured and feasible solution
  • Positive lecturer feedback and strong marks
  • A business idea validated by real student needs

Ways of working

Framework

Kanban methodology supported by Trello to manage tasks and workflow.

Ceremonies

  • Daily stand-up
  • Sprint planning
  • Review / demo
  • Retrospective

Communication

Microsoft Teams and a WhatsApp group. Response within 2–4 hours in academic hours and within 1 hour for urgent matters.

Decision-making

Consensus first. If unresolved, the Agile Lead decides after hearing the team. Escalation goes to the lecturer if needed.

Core values

Respect, transparency, accountability, trust and continuous learning.

Definition of Done

Work is finished and reviewed, accurate, meets the rubric, properly formatted and approved by all team members.

Reflection 10 marks

What the charter meant in practice, and what I learned from it.

What we did

At the start of the project, our team wrote an Agile Team Charter that set out our purpose, roles, ceremonies, working agreements and Definition of Done. I took the role of Analyst / Marketing Lead, which meant my focus was understanding the ICT students that LaunchPad.dev is built for.

How it worked in practice

The charter was not a document we submitted and forgot. We committed to daily stand-ups, and in practice we held daily check-ins on WhatsApp and met every day after our sessions with the lecturer. This kept everyone aware of progress and let us raise blockers early, as our working agreements promised. Because roles were agreed from day one, we always knew who to go to for technical, research, testing or customer questions.

What I learned

I learned that a charter only works when the team actually lives by it. Agreeing on consensus decision-making and clear communication rules reduced confusion and kept us accountable to each other. It also showed me that Agile is as much about people and communication as it is about tools like Trello.
